The story

It began with the books my mother read, and a room that changed everything.

My mother was an avid reader of the shastras. Our home was full of books on Vastu Shastra, Shilpa Kala, Indian architecture and history. Whenever we went to look at a house, we would notice where the sun rose. I grew up listening to these conversations.

When my father passed away following a brain stroke, my grandmother came to live with us. We built a room for her, changing the centre and layout of our home. In our experience, that change brought a new sense of positivity and transformed how our family felt in the space.

That was when I began to explore how the traditional principles of Vastu might influence the way we experience our surroundings. After my daughter was born in 2013, I studied the subject more deeply, completed formal courses in India and continued learning from different institutes. By 2015, Vastu had become my full-time practice.

Today, my approach is attentive, practical and deeply personal: listening first, studying the space carefully and recommending remedies that fit real life.

Why Vastu matters

Every desire you carry through life is quietly connected, in Vastu philosophy, to a zone of your home.

01

The home holds your mind

We spend much of life at home. In Vastu philosophy, our surroundings reflect and influence how we think about family, work, rest, creativity and our future.

02

Sixteen zones, one life

Traditional Vastu associates zones with themes such as opportunities, health, clarity, relationships, financial security, celebration, recognition, stability, savings, children's growth, gains and relaxation.

03

The concern reveals a direction

Every consultation begins with listening. Anupama reviews the client's concerns, how the space is used, the entrance and placements, and a scaled floor plan with directional markings.

04

Remedies, not reconstruction

Depending on the space, guidance may involve colour, placement, tapes or other non-invasive adjustments rather than major building work. More advanced consultations may explore land-energy concepts, including Rajas, Sattva and Tamas.
